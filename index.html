Mediation Analysis: A Practical Guide for Researchers

Mediation analysis is a statistical method used to understand how or why an independent variable influences a dependent variable through a third variable called a mediator.

Understanding the Mediation Model

A basic mediation model includes an independent variable (X), a mediator (M), and an outcome variable (Y). The indirect effect represents the pathway from X through M to Y.

Why Is Mediation Analysis Useful?

Researchers in psychology, management, marketing, and social sciences use mediation analysis to investigate the mechanisms behind relationships between variables. For example, researchers might examine whether customer satisfaction mediates the relationship between service quality and customer loyalty.

How to Calculate and Interpret Results

Researchers typically examine the indirect effect and its confidence interval. Bootstrapping is commonly used to estimate confidence intervals for indirect effects. Interpretation should consider the study design, assumptions, and statistical evidence.

Conclusion

Mediation analysis helps researchers investigate potential explanatory pathways between variables. Understanding the indirect effect is an important step in interpreting a mediation model accurately.
